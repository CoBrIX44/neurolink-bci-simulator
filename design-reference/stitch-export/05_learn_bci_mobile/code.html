Principles of Brain-Computer Interfaces

An engineering primer on cortical electrophysiology, digital filter pipelines, and the boundaries between biological reality and synthetic simulators.

01 // THE PARADIGM

What is a Brain-Computer Interface?

A Brain-Computer Interface (BCI) provides a direct communication pathway between cortical neurons and an external computational system, completely bypassing peripheral nerves and muscles.

Standard Closed-Loop Architecture:
  1. Acquisition: Scalp electrodes convert ionic currents to µV voltages.
  2. Conditioning: ADC + 1–40 Hz Butterworth bandpass.
  3. Feature Extraction: FFT spectral power in target bands.
  4. Classification: Machine learning separating mental classes.
  5. Actuation: Feedback steering neuroprosthetics or cursors.
02 // BIOPHYSICAL ORIGINS

How Scalp EEG Works

Scalp EEG does not record individual action potentials. Instead, it measures summed post-synaptic potentials (PSPs) from thousands of pyramidal neurons aligned perpendicularly to the cerebral cortex.

compare_arrows Real Hardware vs Simulator

Real BCI Systems:
  • Records microvolt signals (5–100 µV) via wet or dry scalp electrodes.
  • Subject to blinks (EOG), heartbeats (ECG), and jaw EMG noise.
  • High biological nonstationarity; requires frequent recalibration.
NeuroLink Simulator:
  • Synthesizes mathematical waveforms in arbitrary units at 256 Hz.
  • Incorporate ~8 Hz (LEFT) and ~22 Hz (RIGHT) frequency proxies.
  • Deterministic random seeds for reproducible classroom study.
  • Zero-phase offline Butterworth filtering via filtfilt.
03 // CLINICAL IMPACT

Assistive Technology Applications

Motor Rehabilitation:

Translating sensorimotor rhythm desynchronization (ERD) into electrical muscle stimulation (FES) or robotic arm control for hemiplegic stroke recovery.

Augmentative Spellers:

P300 and SSVEP matrix communication interfaces enabling speech and typing for patients with ALS or locked-in syndrome.

04 // TECHNICAL LIMITS

Biophysical Constraints

  • Volume Conduction: The resistive skull smears cortical electric fields; localized source localization is ill-posed.
  • Low SNR: Faint cortical potentials are easily buried beneath environmental electromagnetic radiation.
  • Bandwidth: Non-invasive ITR rates typically range between 10–60 bits/min.
